Morpolicy

Zero-Shot Cross-Embodiment Grasping
with a Morphology-aware Policy

Paper Video Code Data

Abstract

Generalizing grasping policies to manipulators with unseen morphologies remains a key challenge in robotic manipulation. While prior work demonstrates that shared postural synergy representations enhance learning efficiency, their generalization to novel manipulators remains constrained. In this paper, we propose Morpolicy, a method to learn a cross-embodiment grasping policy that improves zero-shot generalization to novel manipulators. Building on anchor-based geometric descriptions, Morpolicy introduces a morphology-aware posture synergy space, coupled with a morphology descriptor to disentangle grasp pose from manipulator embodiment. This disentanglement allows easy integration of novel manipulators into the same policy during inference. We evaluate Morpolicy on grasping tasks involving both seen and unseen manipulators and objects, ranging from parallel grippers to multi-fingered dexterous hands. Morpolicy achieves a substantially higher zero-shot grasp success rate on novel manipulators than the baselines. In real-world experiments, Morpolicy achieves an average of 75% success rate on three novel manipulators.
